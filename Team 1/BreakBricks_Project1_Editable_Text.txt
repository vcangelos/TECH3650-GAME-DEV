DESIGN BRIEF

Game and player goal
BreakBricks is a small Red-versus-Blue capture-the-flag experience. The player steals the enemy flag and brings it back to their own flag while their team's flag is still at home. I wanted short team matches that combine crossing an open arena, climbing to rooftop flags, and defending against the other team.

Core mechanic and completion
Players touch an enemy flag to carry it, then touch their own flag to capture. The first team to three captures wins. A round also ends after five minutes: the higher score wins, or equal scores produce a tie. During a ten-second intermission, players receive ForceFields and cannot collect flags. A new round resets captures and moves both flags to their other marked locations. Player spawn pads stay fixed.

Environment and scope
The current arena has two Red bases, two Blue bases, an open central area, cover, and fixed team spawns. Small platforms inside the bases lead toward the roofs. Each team has two hand-placed flag markers. Must-have features are a complete capture loop, team spawns, anchored structures, score feedback, and repeatable rounds. Extra scenery, more flag positions, and more modes are optional. The project uses one CTF server script and the existing ClassicSword tool.

Scripted feature
The flag system starts when a part of a living player's character touches a flag. The server checks the player's team and whether the flag is already carried. For an enemy flag, it attaches the flag to the player's back with a WeldConstraint and records the flag's team in a CarryingFlag Attribute. Touching the player's own flag while it is home awards a capture, increases the team score, and returns the stolen flag. Death or respawning also returns it. The player sees the flag move with them, a harmless explosion effect, and updated leaderboard values. Three team captures end the round and activate intermission protection.

PLAYTESTING AND AI VERIFICATION

Classmate playtest and revision
Classmates played the early game. They pointed out loose, unanchored parts and a bare map, and stressed that the teams needed bases. They liked the idea of flags moving between locations and thought the scope was feasible. I used that feedback to prioritize anchoring and bases. The current build has four color-coded base models with anchored structural parts. These changes give each team a recognizable area and make the level more complete. Tester names and the exact test date were not recorded.

Development testing
An October 7 Codex test of version 37 confirmed pickup, captures, and round reset, but also recorded a nil-parent error in the flag-touch callback. On October 13, Codex inspected version 82 through the Roblox Studio MCP and tested the newer build. Pickup, three captures, win credit, intermission protection, blocked intermission pickups, score reset, changed flag locations, death return, and Spectator rejection worked in the tested session. A later natural five-minute timeout produced a tie, kept wins/losses unchanged, and restarted the round. No script errors appeared in that session's captured Output.

Limits and next revision
The automated capture checks used targeted character positioning; they do not establish multiplayer balance or prove the full rooftop route. Normal movement reached a base interior and the first few platforms, but a later scripted jump failed. My next priorities are clearer rules and round feedback, another ordinary playthrough of the complete climbing route, and cleaning up the remaining loose part.

AI tools and suggestions
I used Claude for CTF scripting and troubleshooting, and ChatGPT/Codex for inspection, testing, and assignment preparation. My earlier requests asked for manually adjustable flag locations, readable code, Attributes instead of a custom state table, and no custom pickup GUI. AI suggested the marker-based flag system and round logic. I requested the Attributes approach and removed the generated pickup GUI from the design.
